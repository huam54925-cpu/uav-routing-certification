\begin{abstract}
We specify a sufficient interface for a supplied family of internal excursions
in a star component separated from the original depot by a non-depot edge.
Every participating route enters that component once and leaves once.
The interface keeps aggregate visits and directed flow, paired crossing
identities and ownership labels, and a cost for each excursion. We give exact
list-concatenation identities, a matrix characterization of realizability,
extraction and reconstruction proofs, and a replacement theorem quantified over
all admitted exterior contexts. Service fees and budgets may be signed rational
numbers. These are paper proofs; no new Lean theorem, general subtree algorithm,
minimality claim, or complexity classification is supplied.
\end{abstract}

\section{Fixed Model and Exact Scope}
\label{sec:model}

Fix a finite simple undirected tree $\mathcal T=(V,E)$, depot $o$, and an edge
$\{x,h\}$ with $o,x,h$ pairwise distinct. Deleting this edge leaves a component
$S$ not containing $o$. Assume
\[
 S=\{h\}\mathbin{\dot\cup} A,
\]
where $A$ is a finite set of leaves adjacent only to $h$.
In particular $x\notin S$ and the only edge from $S$ to its complement is
$\{x,h\}$. The exterior tree is otherwise unrestricted.
Fix a fee function $s:V\to\Q$. No sign condition is imposed on $s$ or the
eventual budget $T\in\Q$.

For a finite vertex list $L$, let $N_L(v)$ count occurrences of $v$, and let
$F_L(u,v)$ count occurrences of $(u,v)$ in $\operatorname{zip}(L,\operatorname{tail}L)$.
A full route $r$ starts and ends at $o$, has at least one intervening vertex,
has no intervening $o$, and has consecutive vertices joined by tree edges.
Its service cost is
\[
 C_s(r)=\sum_{v\ne o}s(v)N_r(v).
\]
The two depot occurrences are not charged, even if $s(o)\ne0$.
The graph-support requirement here is explicit: repository
\src{RouteToFlow}{\code{LegalRoute}} itself only requires unequal adjacent
vertices, not membership in a specified graph.

For fixed demands $d:V\to\N$, flow $f:V\times V\to\N$, and family size $m$,
an exact feasible family has $m$ such routes, aggregate visits $d(v)$ for
$v\ne o$, aggregate directed flow $f(u,v)$ for all $u,v$, and cost at most $T$
on every route. This agrees with
\src{SupportExactFeasibility}{\code{SupportExactFeasibility.ExactFeasible}}
on the constructed tree-supported families. The value $d(o)$ is ignored.
There are no travel charges, time windows, precedence constraints, or
route-specific visit/flow prescriptions in this contract.

\section{Pieces, Crossing Identities, and the Matching Policy}
\label{sec:pieces}

\begin{definition}[Internal witness]
Fix a finite excursion index set $I$. A witness $P=(W_i)_{i\in I}$ is a family
of lists determined by words $w_i=[a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,q_i}]$ over $A$:
\[
 W_i=[h,a_{i,1},h,\ldots,a_{i,q_i},h].
\]
For the empty word the list is $[h]$, with one hub occurrence.
All repetitions of leaves are permitted. Define $n_i(a)=N_{w_i}(a)$.
The completed local walk is $Q_i=[x]\cat W_i\cat[x]$.
\end{definition}

These are partial walks in the original tree, not routes with artificial depot
$h$. Treating them as \code{LegalRoute h} would forbid legitimate intermediate
hub visits.

Give excursion $i$ the two distinct crossing identities
$e_i^+=(i,+)$ and $e_i^-=(i,-)$, directed $x\to h$ and $h\to x$, respectively.
Their pairing is $e_i^+\leftrightarrow e_i^-$.
Each also carries the same excursion identity $i$ and ownership/type label
$\lambda(i)$. These are identities of occurrences, not merely geometric
endpoints. Absolute positions in a full route list are not stored; extraction
locates those positions and assigns the canonical identities.

\begin{definition}[Admitted exterior context]
An exterior context $E_0$ consists of disjoint finite index sets $J$ and $K$,
with $|J|+|K|=m$, and the following structurally validated pieces:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For each $j\in J$, a prefix $U_j$ starting at $o$ and ending at $x$,
 and a suffix $V_j$ starting at $x$ and ending at $o$. Their vertices lie
 outside $S$, their consecutive pairs are tree edges, $o$ occurs in $U_j$
 only at its first position, and in $V_j$ only at its last position.
 \item For each $k\in K$, a full legal tree-supported route $R_k$ avoiding
 $S$. These routes have no slot and will be retained unchanged.
 \item An allowed set $\Gamma_{E_0,\lambda}\subseteq\operatorname{Bij}(J,I)$.
 Its definition may use the slot identities, exterior labels, and retained
 internal ownership labels and paired ports. It must not inspect deleted
 internal order or other data absent from the interface.
\end{enumerate}
There is exactly one slot on each participating route, and no other
participating routes. Budget and aggregate exactness are checked later.
\end{definition}

The permitted set can be a singleton for fixed ownership, all bijections for
fully exchangeable routes, or a subset defined by retained labels. It can be
empty. Matching $j$ to $i$ moves $e_i^+,e_i^-$ and $c_i$ together. A matching of
inward and outward crossings independently is not an admitted operation.
Labels in this first contract constrain ownership only; any further
leaf/vehicle restrictions would require their own realization assumptions.

The full list assigned to a slot under $\pi\in\Gamma_{E_0,\lambda}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gluelist}
 G_j^\pi=U_j\cat W_{\pi(j)}\cat V_j.
\end{equation}
This is ordinary concatenation, without identifying endpoints or adding an
$x\to x$ edge. The last $x$ in $U_j$ and the first $x$ in $V_j$ are distinct
visits separated by the nonempty list $W_{\pi(j)}$.

\section{The Three Exact Accounting Identities}
\label{sec:identities}

For a single slot and excursion define, for $v\ne o$,
\begin{align*}
 N_j^{\rm out}(v)&=N_{U_j}(v)+N_{V_j}(v),&
 N_i^{\rm in}(v)&=N_{W_i}(v),\\
 F_j^{\rm out}(u,v)&=F_{U_j}(u,v)+F_{V_j}(u,v),&
 F_i^{\rm in}(u,v)&=F_{Q_i}(u,v),\\
 b_j&=\sum_{v\ne o}s(v)N_j^{\rm out}(v),&
 c_i&=\sum_{v\ne o}s(v)N_i^{\rm in}(v).
\end{align*}
Internal flow includes both separator crossings; internal visits and fees
exclude $x$. Service and flow intentionally have different boundary ownership.
In particular $c_i$ is not the service cost of all non-depot vertices of $Q_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact concatenation]\label{lem:additive}
For any admitted pieces and any matching, the resulting route satisfies
\begin{align}
 N_{G_j^\pi}(v)&=N_j^{\rm out}(v)+N_{\pi(j)}^{\rm in}(v)
 &&(v\ne o),\label{eq:visits}\\
 F_{G_j^\pi}(u,v)&=F_j^{\rm out}(u,v)+F_{\pi(j)}^{\rm in}(u,v)
 &&(u,v\in V),\label{eq:flow}\\
 C_s(G_j^\pi)&=b_j+c_{\pi(j)}.\label{eq:cost}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Occurrences in a concatenation are the disjoint union of occurrences in the
three lists, proving \eqref{eq:visits}. All three lists are nonempty.
The adjacent pairs consist of the pairs within each list and exactly two
new pairs, from the last vertex of $U_j$ to the first of $W_{\pi(j)}$,
and from the last of $W_{\pi(j)}$ to the first of $V_j$.
Those pairs are $(x,h)$ and $(h,x)$, precisely the extra pairs in $Q_{\pi(j)}$
beyond $W_{\pi(j)}$. This proves \eqref{eq:flow}, including when $W=[h]$.
Multiplying \eqref{eq:visits} by $s(v)$ and summing proves \eqref{eq:cost}.
No inequality or sign assumption is used.
\end{proof}

The star gives the more explicit formulas
\begin{align}
 N_i^{\rm in}(h)&=1+\sum_{a\in A}n_i(a),&
 N_i^{\rm in}(a)&=n_i(a),\label{eq:starvisits}\\
 c_i&=s(h)+\sum_{a\in A}(s(a)+s(h))n_i(a).\label{eq:starcost}
\end{align}
All other internal visits vanish. The internal flow is one on each separator
direction, $n_i(a)$ on each direction of the spoke $\{h,a\}$, and zero
elsewhere. If $w_i=[]$, then $c_i=s(h)$, not $0$ or $2s(h)$.

\section{Interface and a Complete Realizability Criterion}
\label{sec:realization}

\begin{definition}[Summary and realization]
For fixed tree, $s$, $I$, and labels $\lambda$, retain
\[
 \Sigma(P)=(I,\lambda,\{e_i^+\leftrightarrow e_i^-\}_{i\in I},D,F,c),
 \quad D=\sum_i N_i^{\rm in},\quad F=\sum_i F_i^{\rm in},\quad c=(c_i)_{i\in I}.
\]
We extend $D$ by zero at $o$. A candidate summary $\sigma$ has these types,
but need not be realizable. Define $\Real(P,\sigma)$ to mean that $P$ is a
valid witness with the specified labels and $\Sigma(P)=\sigma$.
Define $\operatorname{Realizable}(\sigma)$ by $\exists P\,\Real(P,\sigma)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Star realization iff]\label{thm:realization}
Let $k=|I|$. A candidate summary with the canonical paired ports is realizable
if and only if there are $H:A\to\N$ and $n:I\times A\to\N$ satisfying all
of the following equalities:
\begin{align}
 \sum_{i\in I}n_i(a)&=H(a) &&(a\in A),\label{eq:cols}\\
 D(a)&=H(a) &&(a\in A),\notag\\
 D(h)&=k+\sum_{a\in A}H(a),& D(v)&=0\quad(v\notin S),\label{eq:ledgerD}\\
 F(x,h)&=F(h,x)=k,& F(h,a)&=F(a,h)=H(a)\quad(a\in A),\label{eq:ledgerF}\\
 F(u,v)&=0 &&\text{on all other ordered pairs},\notag\\
 c_i&=s(h)+\sum_{a\in A}(s(a)+s(h))n_i(a) &&(i\in I).\label{eq:rowcost}
\end{align}
There is no local condition $c_i\le T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From a witness take $n_i(a)$ to be its leaf counts and $H(a)$ their column
sums. Equations \eqref{eq:starvisits}--\eqref{eq:starcost} and the explicit
spoke/separator flow counts give every displayed equality.

Conversely, fix an ordering of the finite set $A$. For each row form a word
by repeating $a$ exactly $n_i(a)$ times, in that order, and insert $h$ before,
between, and after those letters. This produces the required $W_i$, including
$[h]$ for a zero row. Every consecutive pair is a spoke edge. Adding the two
separator edges produces $Q_i$ with the required paired crossings. Attach
the prescribed labels to the rows. The displayed equations verify all of
$D,F,c$, so this witness realizes the identical candidate summary.
\end{proof}

Thus an arbitrary rational cost vector, even with the right sum, is not a
certificate of realizability. For example $I=\{1\}$, $A=\{a\}$,
$H(a)=1$, $s(h)=0$, $s(a)=2$ forces $c_1=2$; $c_1=1$ is impossible.
More strongly, with $I=\{1,2\}$ and the same leaf data, $c=(1,1)$ has the
correct total cost $2$ but is unrealizable: its sole unit column must be
$(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$, giving costs $(2,0)$ or $(0,2)$.
Returning to the one-row example with correct cost, $D(h)=1$ instead of $2$, or
$F(x,h)=0$ instead of $1$, is also impossible. Column sums and cost equations
alone would miss these malformed ledgers.

If $I=\varnothing$, \eqref{eq:cols} forces $H=0$ and all ledgers vanish.
If $A=\varnothing$, every row is hub-only: $D(h)=k$, separator flow is $k$
in each direction, and every $c_i=s(h)$. These are different boundary cases.

\begin{corollary}[Finite image for fixed counts and labels]\label{cor:finite}
Fix $I,A,H,s$, labels and the canonical ports. The set of realizable summaries
whose leaf totals equal this fixed $H$ is finite.
For $k=|I|>0$, its cardinality is at most
\[
 \prod_{a\in A}\binom{H(a)+k-1}{k-1}
 \ \le\ \prod_{a\in A}(H(a)+1)^k.
\]
For $k=0$, it has one element if $H=0$, and no elements otherwise.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A column is a weak composition of $H(a)$ into $k$ natural parts. Choosing
columns independently gives the first product, and each of the $k$ entries
lies in $\{0,\ldots,H(a)\}$, giving the second. The summary is an image of
this finite matrix set; different matrices can have identical costs. Empty
columns and empty rows are handled as above.
\end{proof}
This bound concerns fixed finite indexing and fixed labels, not arbitrary
port names or unbounded families. It proves neither a small state space nor
an encoding-length or runtime bound. $D$ and $F$ are deliberately redundant
in this star; retaining both makes the two global exactness checks explicit.

\section{Compatibility, Reconstruction, and Extraction}
\label{sec:main}

Include unchanged routes in the exterior totals:
\begin{align*}
 D^{\rm out}(v)&=\sum_{j\in J}N_j^{\rm out}(v)+\sum_{k\in K}N_{R_k}(v)
 &&(v\ne o),\\
 F^{\rm out}(u,v)&=\sum_{j\in J}F_j^{\rm out}(u,v)+\sum_{k\in K}F_{R_k}(u,v).
\end{align*}
Set $\Compat(\sigma,E_0;d,f,T)$ to mean
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compat}
 \begin{gathered}
 \exists\pi\in\Gamma_{E_0,\lambda},\qquad
 D^{\rm out}(v)+D(v)=d(v)\quad(v\ne o),\\
 F^{\rm out}(u,v)+F(u,v)=f(u,v)\quad(u,v\in V),\\
 b_j+c_{\pi(j)}\le T\quad(j\in J),\qquad
 C_s(R_k)\le T\quad(k\in K).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
An unequal number of excursions and slots makes the bijection set empty.
An explicit realization obligation is separate from this arithmetic predicate.

For a supplied valid $P$, let $\Glue(P,E_0;d,f,T)$ mean that some allowed
matching yields an exact feasible family consisting of the actual lists
in \eqref{eq:gluelist} and the actual unchanged $R_k$.
It does not permit replacing $P$ by a different realization, rerouting the
exterior, or silently reordering a given interior word.

\begin{theorem}[Supplied-witness exact gluing contract]\label{thm:glue}
For every valid $P$ and structurally admitted $E_0$,
\[
 \boxed{\Glue(P,E_0;d,f,T)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Compat(\Sigma(P),E_0;d,f,T).}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose gluing succeeds with $\pi$. Apply Lemma \ref{lem:additive} on each
participating route. Summing and reindexing the interior sums by the bijection
$\pi$ gives the two aggregate equations in \eqref{eq:compat}. Individual
budget bounds give its remaining inequalities, including those for unchanged
routes. This establishes necessity for the same matching.

Conversely choose the matching in \eqref{eq:compat} and construct the lists
\eqref{eq:gluelist}. Each starts and ends at $o$. There is no internal depot:
the pieces exclude it except at their specified outer endpoints, and $S$
excludes it. Each route has a nonempty task sequence because $W_{\pi(j)}$
contains $h$. Within the pieces adjacency holds by assumption; the two new
adjacent pairs are the distinct-vertex tree edges $(x,h)$ and $(h,x)$.
Thus these are legal routes on the original tree. Their number, together
with the unchanged routes, is $|J|+|K|=m$.
Choose any equivalence from the disjoint union $J\sqcup K$ to
$\operatorname{Fin}(m)$ when interfacing with the repository predicate;
finite reindexing changes none of the sums or individual budget claims.
Lemma \ref{lem:additive}, bijective reindexing, and \eqref{eq:compat} give
exact total visits, exact total directed flow, and all route budgets.

Finally every participating route remains in the root branch containing $x$:
all its non-depot vertices form a connected walk in $\mathcal T-\{o\}$
containing $x$. Distinct components of that graph are distinct root branches.
Unchanged legal routes likewise stay within their respective root branches.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Extraction from complete routes]\label{lem:extract}
Suppose an exact feasible tree-supported family is partitioned into $J,K$,
where every $j\in J$ traverses $(x,h)$ exactly once and $(h,x)$ exactly once,
and every $k\in K$ avoids $S$. Suppose also that the supplied ownership
policy admits the resulting pairing of slots with excursion identities.
Then cutting at these crossings yields valid $P,E_0$ with
$\Compat(\Sigma(P),E_0;d,f,T)$ and recovers the original full lists by gluing.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The route begins outside $S$. Since the separator is its only connection to
the complement, its first crossing must be inward and its second outward.
Before the inward crossing and after the outward crossing it stays outside;
between them it stays inside. Otherwise another separator crossing would
be necessary. Cut after the $x$ before entry and before the $x$ after exit.
The resulting pieces are $U_j,W_i,V_j$, with each occurrence retained once.
In a star a walk from $h$ to $h$ alternates the hub with leaves, so $W_i$
has the required form, including the possible singleton $[h]$.
The complete-route assumptions give the external endpoint, support, and
depot-exclusion conditions. Label the two identified crossing occurrences
by $(i,+),(i,-)$; the assumed policy permits their matching.
Concatenation returns the identical original list. Lemma \ref{lem:additive}
and exact feasibility now give compatibility as in Theorem \ref{thm:glue}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform contextual replacement]\label{cor:replace}
Fix the tree, $s,I$, ownership labels, and policy family described in
Section \ref{sec:pieces}. If $P,P'$ are valid and $\Sigma(P)=\Sigma(P')$, then
for every structurally admitted $E_0$ and every $d,f,T$,
\[
 \Glue(P,E_0;d,f,T)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \Glue(P',E_0;d,f,T).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both sides are equivalent to the same compatibility predicate by
Theorem \ref{thm:glue}. The permitted matching sets agree because their
definition only uses retained metadata and the same exterior context.
\end{proof}
The fee function is fixed: equality of cost vectors for one $s$ does not
assert equality for every other fee function. Under an index isomorphism,
labels, paired ports, and costs must all be transported together, and allowed
matchings transported accordingly; an unlabelled cost multiset is insufficient
for an arbitrary fixed-ownership policy.

\begin{corollary}[Abstract-state existence]\label{cor:abstract}
For every structurally admitted exterior context $E_0$ and arbitrary candidate
summary $\sigma$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\exists P\,\bigl(\Real(P,\sigma)\land\Glue(P,E_0;d,f,T)\bigr)\\
 &\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Realizable}(\sigma)\land\Compat(\sigma,E_0;d,f,T).
 \end{aligned}
\]
If the right side holds, every realization of $\sigma$ glues successfully.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use Theorem \ref{thm:glue} and the definition of realization in both directions.
The final statement follows by applying the same theorem to any realization.
\end{proof}
This distinguishes a theorem about each supplied witness from existential
search over all realizations. It does not identify the discarded words or
provide a general recursive state transition.

\section{Boundary Tests and Failure Modes}
\label{sec:tests}

Use the original separator example with edges
$o-x,x-h,h-a,h-b,x-c_1,x-c_2$ and exterior pieces
\[
 U_j=[o,x,c_j,x],\quad V_j=[x,o].
\]
With $s(a)=s(b)=3$, $s(c_j)=4$, and $s(x)=s(h)=0$, the witnesses
\[
 ([h,a,h,b,h],[h])\quad\hbox{and}\quad([h,a,h],[h,b,h])
\]
have identical $D,F$, but respective cost vectors $(6,0)$ and $(3,3)$.
Their full route costs are $(10,4)$ and $(7,7)$. At $T=7$ the first fails
even with all bijections allowed; the second passes. Both produce demands
$d(x)=6,d(h)=4,d(a)=d(b)=d(c_1)=d(c_2)=1$.

Signed fees preserve every equality used here. They invalidate an unsupported
monotone pruning argument, not this final-budget contract. Unlike decomposition
at a depot-root branch, the two pieces belong to the same route and can cancel
each other's signed costs. Other root branches cannot contribute such a
cancellation to that route.
