\begin{abstract}
Two small extensions separate the universal concatenation argument from the
star-specific realization argument. First, the exact gluing contract for given
legal internal walks remains valid when the internal component is an arbitrary
subtree behind a single edge, provided each participating route enters and
leaves exactly once. Second, when every matching of excursions to exterior
slots is allowed, the remaining budget test depends only on the multiset of
internal costs: decreasing exterior costs must be paired with increasing
internal costs. Both results allow signed rational fees and budgets. These are
paper proofs with finite regression checks, not Lean formalizations, a general
summary-realizability theorem, or a recursive tree algorithm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Package A: Given Walks in an Arbitrary Internal Subtree}
\label{sec:package-a}

Fix a finite simple undirected tree $\mathcal T=(V,E)$, depot $o$, and an edge
$\{x,h\}$, with $o,x,h$ pairwise distinct. Let $S$ be the component of
$\mathcal T-\{\{x,h\}\}$ containing $h$, and assume $o\notin S$.
Thus $x\notin S$, and $\{x,h\}$ is the only edge from $S$ to $V\setminus S$.
There is no restriction on the shape of the induced subtree on $S$.
Fix a service fee $s:V\to\Q$ and a budget $T\in\Q$.

For a finite vertex list $L$, let $N_L(v)$ count occurrences of $v$, and let
$F_L(u,v)$ count consecutive ordered pairs $(u,v)$ in $L$. Put
\[
 C_s(L)=\sum_{v\ne o}s(v)N_L(v).
\]
Even for a partial list this definition ignores depot occurrences. A full
route starts and ends at $o$, has at least one intervening vertex, contains no
other occurrence of $o$, and has every consecutive pair in the tree's edge
relation. In particular, the support condition is stronger than merely
requiring unequal consecutive vertices. For targets $d:V\to\N$ and
$f:V\times V\to\N$, an exact feasible family of size $m$ has aggregate visits
$d(v)$ for $v\ne o$, aggregate flow $f(u,v)$ for all ordered pairs, and cost at
most $T$ on each route. The value $d(o)$ is immaterial. No travel costs,
precedence conditions, or individual route visit targets are added.

\begin{definition}[Supplied internal witness and retained interface]
\label{def:interface}
Let $I$ be a finite excursion index set. A valid witness $P=(W_i)_{i\in I}$
consists of nonempty vertex lists contained in $S$, starting and ending at $h$,
whose consecutive vertices are adjacent in the original tree. In particular,
$W_i=[h]$ is allowed. Other visits to $h$ are allowed; these walks are not
routes with an artificial depot at $h$. Define
\begin{align*}
 Q_i&=[x]\cat W_i\cat[x], & c_i&=C_s(W_i),\\
 D_P(v)&=\sum_{i\in I}N_{W_i}(v), &
 F_P(u,v)&=\sum_{i\in I}F_{Q_i}(u,v).
\end{align*}
The two boundary occurrences of $x$ belong to the external route pieces;
they are not counted in $D_P$ or charged in $c_i$.

The interface $\Sigma(P)$ retains $I,D_P,F_P,(c_i)_{i\in I}$, and metadata
$\lambda$. This metadata includes distinct directed ports $(i,+)$ and $(i,-)$
at $x\to h$ and $h\to x$, paired with each other, and any ownership or type
labels that constrain matching. Ports identify crossing occurrences, not
absolute positions in the eventual route list. An excursion, its cost, its
two ports, and its labels move together. All other internal order is deleted.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Exterior context and permitted matching]
\label{def:context}
An exterior context $\mathcal E$ consists of finite sets $J$ of slots and $K$
of unchanged routes, with $|J|+|K|=m$. A slot $j$ has a prefix $U_j$ from $o$
to $x$ and a suffix $V_j$ from $x$ to $o$. Both lists stay in $V\setminus S$
and are tree-supported; $o$ occurs only at the first position of $U_j$ and
only at the last position of $V_j$. Each $R_\ell$, $\ell\in K$, is a full
legal tree-supported route avoiding $S$. Set
\begin{align*}
 b_j&=C_s(U_j)+C_s(V_j),\\
 D_{\mathcal E}&=\sum_{j\in J}(N_{U_j}+N_{V_j})
                 +\sum_{\ell\in K}N_{R_\ell},\\
 F_{\mathcal E}&=\sum_{j\in J}(F_{U_j}+F_{V_j})
                 +\sum_{\ell\in K}F_{R_\ell}.
\end{align*}
Function sums are pointwise. In particular $F_{U_j}+F_{V_j}$ is used, not
$F_{U_j\cat V_j}$: the latter would insert a spurious $x\to x$ pair.

The matching policy is a set
$\Gamma_{\mathcal E,\lambda}\subseteq\Bij(J,I)$. It depends only on the
retained metadata and the context, and never on deleted order inside the
$W_i$. It assigns both paired ports to the same slot. The policy may be empty
and need not allow every bijection. For $\pi\in\Gamma_{\mathcal E,\lambda}$,
the glued participating route is the ordinary list concatenation
\[
 G_j^\pi=U_j\cat W_{\pi(j)}\cat V_j.
\]
The two copies of $x$ are separated by a nonempty $W_{\pi(j)}$ and remain two
different visits. No endpoint is identified or discarded in this expression.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The three exact identities]
\label{lem:identities}
For every valid witness, context, permitted matching, and slot $j$,
\begin{align}
 N_{G_j^\pi}(v)&=N_{U_j}(v)+N_{W_{\pi(j)}}(v)+N_{V_j}(v),
                    && v\in V,\label{eq:visits}\\
 F_{G_j^\pi}(u,v)&=F_{U_j}(u,v)+F_{Q_{\pi(j)}}(u,v)+F_{V_j}(u,v),
                    && u,v\in V,\label{eq:flow}\\
 C_s(G_j^\pi)&=b_j+c_{\pi(j)}.\label{eq:cost}
\end{align}
Each $G_j^\pi$ is a full legal tree-supported route. Its nondepot vertices
lie in one component of the tree with $o$ removed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Ordinary list concatenation adds occurrence counts, giving
\eqref{eq:visits}. The only consecutive pairs joining different lists are
$x\to h$ and $h\to x$. Thus the middle contribution to flow is exactly
$F_{W_{\pi(j)}}+\mathbf 1_{(x,h)}+\mathbf 1_{(h,x)}=F_{Q_{\pi(j)}}$,
including the case $W_{\pi(j)}=[h]$. This proves \eqref{eq:flow}.
Multiplying \eqref{eq:visits} by $s(v)$ and summing over $v\ne o$ gives
\eqref{eq:cost}; no sign assumption is used.

The two join edges belong to the tree, all other consecutive edges are legal
inside the supplied pieces, and the internal vertices exclude $o$. There is
at least the visit to $x$ and the nonempty internal walk between the two depot
endpoints. Hence the route is legal. Removing its two depot endpoints leaves
a connected walk in $\mathcal T-\{o\}$, containing $x$, so it lies in a
single root branch. None of these arguments uses a star structure.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility and gluing]
Write $\Compat(\mathcal E,\Sigma(P);d,f,T)$ if there exists
$\pi\in\Gamma_{\mathcal E,\lambda}$ such that
\begin{align}
 D_{\mathcal E}(v)+D_P(v)&=d(v) &&(v\ne o),\label{eq:target-visits}\\
 F_{\mathcal E}(u,v)+F_P(u,v)&=f(u,v) &&(u,v\in V),\label{eq:target-flow}\\
 b_j+c_{\pi(j)}&\le T &&(j\in J),\label{eq:slot-budget}\\
 C_s(R_\ell)&\le T &&(\ell\in K).\label{eq:unchanged-budget}
\end{align}
Write $\Glue(\mathcal E,P;d,f,T)$ if some permitted matching gives an exact
feasible family consisting of the glued routes and the unchanged routes.
This existential assertion concerns the supplied witness $P$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Given-witness gluing iff]
\label{thm:glue}
For every valid $P$ and $\mathcal E$,
\[
 \Glue(\mathcal E,P;d,f,T)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Compat(\mathcal E,\Sigma(P);d,f,T).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed permitted $\pi$, sum \eqref{eq:visits} and \eqref{eq:flow} over
$j$. Bijectivity of $\pi$ replaces the sums indexed by $\pi(j)$ with the sums
over $I$. Adding the unchanged routes gives exactly
\eqref{eq:target-visits} and \eqref{eq:target-flow}. The route budgets are
exactly \eqref{eq:slot-budget} and \eqref{eq:unchanged-budget} by
\eqref{eq:cost}. Legality and size $m$ are already guaranteed by the context
and Lemma~\ref{lem:identities}. Hence feasibility for this matching is
equivalent to the four displayed conditions. Taking the existential
quantifier over the same policy proves both implications. If an indexing by
$\operatorname{Fin}(m)$ is required, reindex the family on $J\sqcup K$ by any
bijection; all sums and route budgets are preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Extraction across the single cut edge]
\label{thm:extraction}
Let an exact feasible family be tree-supported. Suppose each route either
avoids $S$, or traverses $x\to h$ exactly once and $h\to x$ exactly once.
Then every route of the second kind has a unique decomposition
$U_j\cat W_i\cat V_j$ of the form in Definitions~\ref{def:interface}
and~\ref{def:context}, obtained at these two crossing occurrences. It
therefore determines a valid internal witness and exterior context. If the
chosen metadata and policy admit this extracted pairing, their interface is
compatible with the original targets and budget, and reconstruction recovers
the original route lists.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A route begins and ends outside $S$. Since the only cut edge is $\{x,h\}$,
its first crossing must be $x\to h$ and its last crossing must be $h\to x$.
The two assumed crossings are all its crossings. Consequently all its
positions in $S$ form one nonempty interval, beginning and ending at $h$.
Take this interval as $W_i$, the prefix ending at the preceding $x$ as $U_j$,
and the suffix beginning at the following $x$ as $V_j$. The interval is a
tree-supported internal walk, and the exterior pieces avoid $S$ with the
required depot occurrences. The cut positions fix the decomposition uniquely.
All other routes form $K$. Rejoining the three consecutive list pieces
recovers each original list exactly. If the extracted matching is admitted,
Theorem~\ref{thm:glue} gives compatibility. No alternating hub--leaf word is
used in this extraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Same-interface contextual replacement]
\label{cor:replacement}
Let $P$ and $P'$ be valid internal witnesses for the same tree, cut edge,
fee function, index set, and metadata. If $\Sigma(P)=\Sigma(P')$, then for
every admitted exterior context, targets, and budget,
\[
 \Glue(\mathcal E,P;d,f,T)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Glue(\mathcal E,P';d,f,T).
\]
The assertion also holds after a fixed renaming of excursion indices and
their attached ports, costs, and labels.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The policy is unchanged because it depends only on retained metadata.
All four compatibility conditions agree, including the indexed cost of every
matched excursion. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:glue} on both sides. For a renaming,
transport each matching through the same index bijection.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What has not been generalized]
\label{rem:realization}
For an abstract summary $\sigma$, define
$\Real(P,\sigma)$ to mean that $P$ is valid and $\Sigma(P)=\sigma$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:glue} does not supply such a $P$. A claim about abstract
summaries must still include $\exists P\,\Real(P,\sigma)$; checking only the
displayed compatibility equations cannot remove that obligation.

In particular, the earlier star formula
$N_{W_i}(h)=1+\sum_a n_i(a)$ and its matrix realization proof do not extend
to arbitrary subtrees. In the internal path $h-u-a$, the valid walk
\[
 W=[h,u,a,u,a,u,h]
\]
has $N_W(h)=2$, $N_W(u)=3$, and $N_W(a)=2$. The star formula based on the
leaf $a$ would incorrectly give $N_W(h)=3$. Its service cost is
$2s(h)+3s(u)+2s(a)$; its flow on $h\leftrightarrow u$ is once in each
direction, and on $u\leftrightarrow a$ twice in each direction. The actual
walk and its actual ledger do satisfy all the general gluing identities.
Neither multiple boundary excursions in one route nor a general recursive
state transition is covered by this note.
\end{remark}

\section{Package B: Unrestricted Matching of Signed Costs}
\label{sec:package-b}

\begin{theorem}[Exact sorted budget criterion]
\label{thm:sorted}
Let $k\in\N$, let $b_1\ge\cdots\ge b_k$ and
$c_1\le\cdots\le c_k$ be rational sequences, and let $T\in\Q$.
Writing $\mathfrak S_k$ for the permutations of $\{1,\ldots,k\}$,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \exists\pi\in\mathfrak S_k\ \forall j,\ b_j+c_{\pi(j)}\le T
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall j,\ b_j+c_j\le T.
 \quad}
\]
No nonnegativity or strict-order assumption is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For sufficiency choose the identity permutation. For necessity fix a
feasible $\pi$ and $1\le j\le k$. The $j$ distinct images of
$\{1,\ldots,j\}$ cannot all lie in $\{1,\ldots,j-1\}$, which has only
$j-1$ elements. Thus there is an $i\le j$ with $\pi(i)\ge j$. Monotonicity
of the two sorted sequences implies
\[
 b_j+c_j\ \le\ b_i+c_{\pi(i)}\ \le\ T.
\]
This proves each required inequality directly. It uses only the order and
addition of rational numbers, so negative entries and ties cause no change.
For $k=0$, there is exactly one empty permutation, and both families of
inequalities are vacuous; the equivalence still holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Minimum possible largest matched cost]
\label{cor:minimax}
For $k>0$ and the same sorted sequences,
\[
 \min_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_k}\ \max_{1\le j\le k}
       (b_j+c_{\pi(j)})
 =\max_{1\le j\le k}(b_j+c_j).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For any $\pi$ take $T=\max_j(b_j+c_{\pi(j)})$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:sorted}. Every $b_j+c_j$ is at most this maximum, so their
maximum is a lower bound on every matching's maximum. The identity matching
attains the bound. Both finite sets of maxima are nonempty because $k>0$.
No undefined maximum of an empty set is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cost multiset suffices for the remaining budget test]
\label{cor:multiset}
In Package A, suppose $|J|=|I|=k$ and the policy permits every bijection:
$\Gamma_{\mathcal E,\lambda}=\Bij(J,I)$. Sort the exterior costs decreasingly
as $b_1,\ldots,b_k$ and the internal costs increasingly as
$c_1,\ldots,c_k$. The supplied witness glues if and only if
\eqref{eq:target-visits}, \eqref{eq:target-flow}, and
\eqref{eq:unchanged-budget} hold, and
\[
 b_j+c_j\le T\qquad(1\le j\le k).
\]
For fixed structural ledgers and fee function, with all such matchings
admitted, the multiset of internal costs therefore determines the remaining
budget feasibility. In particular, two valid witnesses with equal structural
ledgers and equal cost multisets have identical gluing feasibility in every
such unrestricted exterior context.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The visit and flow equations do not depend on the bijection. The unchanged
routes' budgets do not depend on it either. Sorting both finite sequences
only renames the slots and excursions, and the policy allows all bijections
before and after these renamings. The remaining existential budget condition
is exactly Theorem~\ref{thm:sorted}; then use Theorem~\ref{thm:glue}.
The sorted internal sequence is determined by the cost multiset, including
multiplicities. Excursions and their two crossing ports are matched as whole
objects throughout.
\end{proof}

The universal-matching hypothesis is essential. If ownership forces the
identity matching, take exterior costs $(6,0)$, indexed internal costs
$(2,1)$, and $T=7$. The forced sums are $(8,1)$, so the budget fails.
Free matching instead pairs costs $(1,2)$ with $(6,0)$ and gives $(7,2)$,
which passes. Thus a retained label constraint cannot be silently dropped.
These internal costs can occur with one fixed fee function: let $s(h)=1$
and $s(a)=0$ on the internal edge $h-a$; the valid walks $[h]$ and
$[h,a,h]$ have costs $1$ and $2$. Swapping their assigned indices preserves
the aggregate structural ledger and cost multiset, but changes the indexed
costs seen by an identity-only policy.

The multiset result concerns the budget matching of already given witnesses.
It proves neither realizability of an arbitrary internal cost vector nor
complete representability of an arbitrary subtree by a cost multiset. Route
type restrictions, fixed ownership, or any other proper subset of allowed
bijections require the actual constrained matching problem.

\section{Boundary Cases and Verification Evidence}
\label{sec:evidence}

The empty witness family and empty slot family are allowed: there is a unique
empty bijection if the policy includes it, and only the unchanged routes'
budgets and aggregate target equations remain. A policy excluding that
bijection is empty and makes gluing false. A singleton internal walk $[h]$
contributes one visit to $h$, both cut-edge directions once, and cost $s(h)$.
Signed costs must be retained exactly: an internal cost $10$ and exterior
cost $-4$ meet budget $7$, despite the internal cost exceeding $7$. No local
discarding rule based on an internal cost alone is implied. Repeated visits
inside $S$ are allowed, but the legal route $[o,x,h,x,h,x,o]$ crosses the cut
edge twice each way and lies outside the extraction theorem.

added to Lean, and no PDF compilation was performed. An independent finite
